# NV²D Protocol

**No Visual or Verbal Diarrhea.** The squared 2 covers copy and visuals.

NV²D is a KSD working protocol, not an industry standard. It turns verified company inputs and locked brand direction into concise advertising. It cannot make a model accurate, consistent, rights-safe, or publish-ready by itself.

## Required company inputs

A task is **BLOCKED** until these are supplied:

| Input | Required detail |
|---|---|
| Company and product | Legal company, public brand, exact product |
| Audience and job | Who it serves and what they need to do |
| Product truth | Current capabilities, limits, availability, proof |
| Claim evidence | What may be stated or shown; source and status |
| Channel and action | Placement, dimensions, audience action |
| Locked brand | Approved marks, colors, type, layout, product identity |
| Rights and privacy | Rights for supplied assets, people, voices, data, and references |
| Disclosure rules | Required AI, sponsorship, product, or legal labels for the channel |
| Owner | Person who approves the final copy, image, video, and release |

Missing facts stay missing. No guessed features, results, price, integrations, launch status, customer evidence, contact path, or visual identity.

## Complaint controls

| Commonly reported problem | NV²D control |
|---|---|
| Generic, low-quality work that feels unlike the brand | Lock audience, product promise, proof, visual rules, and voice before generation. Reject stock-looking output that adds no product meaning. |
| Brand drift between tools, formats, or campaign assets | Keep one approved brand record and asset set. Check every output against it; review a contact sheet across formats before release. |
| Too much prompt tweaking; output still needs heavy editing | Generate only the requested deliverable. Change one variable per revision, keep approved versions, and record the human edit still required. |
| Image text, marks, product details, or claims are wrong | Do not trust generated text or logos. Add approved marks and final copy as real design layers; compare depicted product and claims with current evidence. |
| Video subjects, objects, scenes, or motion change between shots | Lock subject, wardrobe, product, setting, and shot order. Approve a frame or storyboard per shot, then inspect continuity and every final cut. |
| Unclear rights, consent, or source provenance | Record the source and permission for each supplied reference, asset, person, and voice. Stop if rights are unknown. Never claim that a model's training data is cleared without evidence. |
| AI use feels deceptive or replaces a real person without consent | Disclose material AI use when required by platform, law, or campaign policy. Require documented consent for synthetic use of an identifiable person's face or voice. |
| Final work ships without human judgment | A named owner reviews factual accuracy, brand fit, rights, safety, accessibility, disclosure, and final export. AI output is a draft until approved. |

These controls reduce preventable failures; they do not guarantee model output or grant legal clearance. Escalate rights, disclosure, and likeness questions for qualified review.

## Output limits

- One headline: six words or fewer.
- One product description: one sentence, 30 words or fewer.
- Supporting copy only for a concrete use, proof, status, privacy, or safety fact.
- One call to action, only if it works.
- One product or benefit per frame.
- No duplicate labels, filler, invented detail, or unapproved logo redraw.
- For video: one approved shot list; no unreviewed generated speech, captions, marks, or product claims.

## Release gate

Approve only when all are true:

1. The company inputs are complete and traceable.
2. The copy says what the product does in one clear sentence.
3. Every spoken, written, and visual claim matches current evidence.
4. Each image and video frame follows the locked brand and continuity sheet.
5. Assets, likenesses, and voices have recorded rights or consent.
6. Required disclosures, accessibility checks, and human approval are complete.
7. The final files are the files reviewed.

Otherwise revise or mark **BLOCKED**. Publication still needs the normal independent review and release approval.

## Research basis

These are recurring risk signals, not a ranked global complaint list. Surveys use different audiences and methods.

- Gartner surveyed 307 U.S. consumers in March 2026; 49% said GenAI had made content quality worse. [Gartner, June 9, 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-09-gartner-survey-finds-49-of-u-s-consumers-say-genai-has-made-content-quality-worse)
- Adobe's June 2026 study surveyed nearly 2,000 creatives across the U.S., U.K., and Japan. It reports high concerns about IP, consent, and training data; 41% were in its “Pragmatic Middle.” [Adobe Research](https://www.adobe.com/ai/research/202606/how-creatives-are-thinking-about-ai.html)
- Canada's 2023–24 copyright consultation received about 1,000 questionnaire responses; many creators raised consent, credit, compensation, and transparency concerns. It reports stakeholder views, not a representative public poll. [ISED Canada](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/strategic-policy-sector/en/marketplace-framework-policy/consultation-copyright-age-generative-artificial-intelligence-what-we-heard-report)
- A 2026 branding-platform study (50 participants) identifies fragmented tools and resulting workflow and visual inconsistency; its small usability study is directional, not a market-wide prevalence estimate. [Archives of Design Research](https://doi.org/10.15187/adr.2026.02.39.1.199)
- Adobe Research's 2025 long-video survey describes continuing character and scene consistency problems in longer generated video. [Adobe Research / ICCV workshop](https://research.adobe.com/publication/a-survey-on-long-video-storytelling-generation-architectures-consistency-and-cinematic-quality/)
- IAB and Sonata Insights surveyed 500+ Gen Z and Millennial consumers and 100 advertising executives; their 2026 report finds consumers less positive about AI ads than executives expect and reports that disclosure can improve perception. [IAB, 2026](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-gap-widens/)
