WHAT WE BELIEVE

Ask for the fact.
Not the life behind the fact.

Prove what matters.

If a service needs one fact, it should not automatically receive your entire personal or financial history.

Keep the rest private.

Privacy is not secrecy. It is relevance. Collect only what is actually required.

Measure the right thing.

If the question is rent reliability, measure rent reliability. Stop using unrelated history as a shortcut.

Make digital access affordable.

The future is going digital. Participation should not depend on owning a $1,000+ smartphone.

Bank Statement Privacy Reader

“Print your bank statements for the last two months” can expose where you shop, what you buy, medical spending, debt, subscriptions, donations, travel and more — even when the requester only needs one narrow fact.

The old way: disclose the document.
The new way: disclose the answer.

KSD Connect

A low-cost secure access device being explored for people who need the essentials of digital life without the price of a modern smartphone.

Current direction: wallet access, privacy proofs, communications, credentials, transit support and low-bandwidth connectivity. A borrowed phone, library computer or kiosk can provide the bigger screen and internet connection — while sensitive approvals stay on the KSD Connect itself.

Borrow the screen. Never borrow the trust.

Current principle: KSD intends to make zero profit on the physical hardware. The goal is access, not hardware margin.

WHY I SAY “US”

I’m not documenting the comeback.
I’m documenting the part before anyone knows if there will be one.

I served in the Canadian Armed Forces and deployed to Afghanistan in 2006. I live with PTSD connected to that service.

For years I had a demanding career, conventional banking and enough income that many ordinary systems simply worked for me. I did not have to think very much about the people those systems leave behind.

Then my circumstances changed.

Health, benefits, housing, credit assumptions, administrative rules, cash access and identity requirements suddenly looked very different from the other side.

I found myself asking the same question over and over: Why did I not know about this earlier?

The uncomfortable answer was simple: because I did not have to know.

Now I do. And I’m building Knight Shield Digital while living through some of the same broken systems this movement is questioning.
